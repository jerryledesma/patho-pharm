1 · why pH matters

she asked: "What does pH stand for?" → power / potential of hydrogen
she asked: "pH is measuring which ion?" → hydrogen (H⁺) — every time she said acidosis.

more H⁺ → lower pH → ACID
less H⁺ → higher pH → BASE

Blood is held in a window of only 0.10. Outside it: enzymes denature, cell reactions slow, permeability & structure change.

pH matters for → enzyme activity · cardiac function · neuro function · cellular metabolism.

body runs slightly alkaline:
20 : 1 bicarb : carbonic acid
metabolism makes acid nonstop, so we keep a base reserve
0  acid
7 neutral
14 base
7.35–7.45
blood
FYI only — she will not test: stomach very acidic (pepsin), skin slightly acidic, small intestine alkaline (trypsin/lipase/amylase); pH <6.8 or >7.8 usually fatal.

2 · acids, bases, buffers

ACID
donates H⁺
HCl, acetic, citric
BASE
accepts / binds H⁺
baking soda, Tums, ammonia
BUFFER
acts as either → resists pH change
"a sponge, a gatekeeper"
the bicarbonate–carbonic acid buffer (PRIMARY · lungs + kidneys · "most important to remember")
CO₂ + H₂O  ⇄  H₂CO₃  ⇄  H⁺ + HCO₃⁻
add acid → H⁺ grabs HCO₃⁻ → carbonic acid → CO₂ + H₂O → exhaled. fewer free H⁺ → pH doesn't crash
add base → carbonic acid splits → releases H⁺ to neutralize it
✎ her pearl: treat CO₂ and acid as synonyms. more CO₂ on board = more acid = lower pH
FYI only (no exam questions): hemoglobin buffer binds H⁺ in low-O₂ tissue, releases it in the lungs · protein buffers (–NH₂, –COOH groups) act as weak acids/bases inside cells.

she asked: "Why arterial and not venous?" → arterial blood (more O₂) gives the accurate picture of oxygenation and CO₂. "A know slide for sure — questions will be asked."

4 · acidosis vs alkalosis · the electrolyte swap

ACIDOSIS pH < 7.35
too much acid made or not enough eliminated → H⁺ piles up
ALKALOSIS pH > 7.45
too much base or base not eliminated ("alkaline" = "base")
she asked: "Hydrogen is not behaving — what's potassium doing?" · "Because of which ion do we watch the EKG?" → potassium
ACIDOSIS
H⁺ in →← K⁺ out
⇒ HYPERkalemia
⇒ HYPERcalcemia (H⁺ takes the albumin seats, Ca⁺⁺ set free)
K⁺ → EKG changes
"lethal injection is potassium"
ALKALOSIS
← H⁺ outK⁺ in →
⇒ HYPOkalemia
⇒ HYPOcalcemia (albumin seats open, Ca⁺⁺ binds → tetany, tingling)
"hyper & hyper · hypo & hypo"
K⁺ high, Ca⁺⁺ high · K⁺ low, Ca⁺⁺ low. Sodium column = don't worry about it.
treatment trap ⚠
fix the acidosis (or give insulin in DKA) → K⁺ follows glucose into the cell → now watch for hypokalemia

5 · respiratory ACIDOSIS

she asked: "One word for respiratory disorders?" → VENTILATION — rate & depth of breathing, "exclusively a lung function." · "Who retains CO₂?" → COPD / emphysema.
HYPOventilationCO₂ trapped
hypercapniaCO₂ + H₂O → carbonic acid → H⁺pH ↓
ABG: pH ↓   PaCO₂ ↑   HCO₃⁻ normal (until kidneys compensate)
signs & symptoms
  • headache, blurred vision, restless → lethargic, twitches, seizures, coma
  • RR rapid at first, then depressed as the center adapts to CO₂
  • warm, flushed skin (vasodilation) — "all red and flushed"
  • K⁺ ↑ · Ca⁺⁺ ↑ → EKG
DEPRES "depressed, no S at the end"
  • DDrugs / oversedation — fentanyl, Versed, opioids
  • EEdema — pulmonary edema, drowning
  • PPneumonia, severe lung infection
  • RRespiratory alteration — stroke (brainstem)
  • EEmphysema, chronic lung disease
  • SSpasm of bronchioles — asthma, COPD
also on the slide: respiratory-muscle / chest-wall disorders (Guillain-Barré — "the muscles just don't work"), inadequate mechanical ventilation.
COPD pearl (said 4×)
COPDers run on a hypoxic drive — adapted to low O₂ / high CO₂. Resident wants 4 L or a non-rebreather on a 2-L patient? "You'll get him in trouble."
her clinic pts: 82–85% sats; "you won't get them above 90, maybe 92." expect a fully compensated respiratory acidosis.
nursing interventions
  • monitor O₂ (COPD caveat!) · give O₂ as appropriate
  • EKG + K⁺ (hyperkalemia) — labs q6h–daily
  • corticosteroids (prednisone, methylpred) for inflammation
  • antibiotics for infection
  • monitor & repeat ABGs
  • naloxone for post-op sedation / overdose
kidneys compensate: keep HCO₃⁻, dump H⁺ — slowly

6 · respiratory ALKALOSIS

she asked: "Blow off CO₂ — what happens to CO₂? to pH?" → CO₂ low, pH high · "Remember ventilation and you'll get it." · PE patient → "how will they be breathing?" → fast, panicked.
HYPERventilationCO₂ blown off
hypocapnialess carbonic acidpH ↑
ABG: pH ↑   PaCO₂ ↓   HCO₃⁻ normal
signs & symptoms
  • dizziness, confusion
  • tingling extremities, tetany, carpopedal spasm ← low Ca⁺⁺
  • cerebral vasoconstriction, seizures, coma
  • K⁺ ↓ · Ca⁺⁺ ↓
"I've had to breathe with them — do I need to bring bags?"
TACHYPNEA
  • TTemperature ↑ — fever, "blowing off infection"
  • AAspirin toxicity
  • CControlled / excessive mechanical ventilation
  • HHyperventilation
  • YYelp / help — anxiety, fear, pain; early sepsis
  • PPneumothorax — "you just took out that lung"
  • NNeurologic disorders; liver failure toxins
  • EEmbolism — PE, "a big one" (may be back pain, not chest)
  • AAltitude — hypoxemia
REST nursing care
  • RRebreather / slow the breathing — coach diaphragmatic breathing, therapeutic communication, grounding, social work
  • EElectrolytes — correct K⁺ & Ca⁺⁺, watch EKG
  • SSedative / anti-anxiety — sometimes IV Ativan
  • TTeach relaxation & breathing for next time
respiratory summary slide
acidosis = HYPOventilation → retain CO₂ → PaCO₂ high → pH low

alkalosis = HYPERventilation → blow off CO₂ → PaCO₂ low → pH high
a hyperventilation episode is "a warning event — it's going to happen again." kidneys compensate slowly: excrete HCO₃⁻, keep H⁺.

7 · metabolic ACIDOSIS

she asked: "Why lose bicarb with diarrhea?" → bicarb deficit → H⁺ excess → metabolic acidosis — "NCLEX question, and we will ask you." · "What are the lungs doing in acidosis?" → hyperventilate, blow off CO₂ · "Laxative user?" → diarrhea → lose bicarb.

too much acid made, or bicarb lost, or kidney can't regenerate it → buffer overwhelmed, ratio < 20:1

ABG: pH ↓   HCO₃⁻ ↓   PaCO₂ normal (falls as lungs compensate)
signs & symptoms
  • headache, lethargy → coma
  • Kussmaul respirations — deep & rapid, blowing off CO₂ (DKA classic)
  • anorexia, N/V, diarrhea, abd pain
  • K⁺ ↑ · Ca⁺⁺ ↑ → EKG
ACIDS (slide spells out A-C-I-D only)
  • AAccumulation of lactate — lactic acidosis, sepsis
  • CChronic diarrhea
  • IImpaired renal function
  • DDiabetic ketoacidosis
↑ acid↓ HCO₃⁻
lactic acid / sepsis · ketoacidosis (DKA, starvation) · ingestion: salicylates, ammonium chloride, ethylene glycol, methanol · renal failure (can't excrete H⁺)diarrhea · hyperkalemia · renal failure (HCO₃⁻ loss) · intestinal fistulas · certain meds
FYI only — anion gap = (Na⁺+K⁺) − (Cl⁻+HCO₃⁻), normal 3–11; MUDPILES is for the residents. "Not on the exam, not on NCLEX."
nursing interventions / treatment
  • monitor breathing — Kussmaul is a sign, don't suppress it
  • treat the underlying cause
  • electrolytes: hyperkalemia now → hypokalemia once treated
  • neuro status, vitals
  • IV fluids: Lactated Ringer's — liver turns lactate → bicarb
  • sodium bicarb only severe / emergency — "can revert fast"
  • EKG monitoring (K⁺)
  • hemodialysis for renal failure — help prep the patient
DKA + insulin ✎
glucose goes into the cell → potassium follows → serum K⁺ drops → hypokalemia
"when you try to fix something, it's very easy to throw this delicate balance"

lungs compensate fast: hyperventilate, blow off CO₂

8 · metabolic ALKALOSIS

she asked: "Suctioning or vomiting — what are they losing?" → hydrochloric acid (H⁺) → bicarb in excess. "Julian did it — suctioning." · "Bicarb high, acid low — pH?" → above 7.45.

acid lost (most common) or base gained

ABG: pH ↑   HCO₃⁻ ↑   PaCO₂ normal (rises as lungs compensate)
signs & symptoms
  • weakness, muscle cramps, hyperactive reflexes
  • tetany — Ca⁺⁺ binds plasma proteins
  • confusion, seizures, tachycardia
  • RR ↓ — retaining CO₂ (compensation you can see)
  • K⁺ ↓ · Ca⁺⁺ ↓
↓ acid / acid loss (most common)↑ HCO₃⁻ (ingestion)
vomiting · NG suction (lose HCl) · loop diuretics, steroids · hyperaldosteronism · hypokalemia · hypochloremiaantacids ("too much Tums") · bicarbonate · lactate given in dialysis
nursing interventions / treatment
  • assess fluid volume & respiratory status
  • treat the cause
  • electrolytes + vitals
  • IV fluid of choice: normal saline
  • IV KCl for hypokalemia — see the sticky ⚠
  • EKG monitoring
lungs compensate fast: hypoventilate, keep CO₂ → pH falls
IV potassium — NCLEX ⚠
never push it.
max 10 mEq / hour, slowly, over an hour
expect a BMP within ~6 h to see if it worked
watch the EKG

9 · ROME & compensation

she asked: "T/F — bicarb and pH trend together?" → true (Metabolic Equal) · "Which friend compensates faster?" → lungs · "If compensated, what's the pH?" → normal.
ROME
Respiratory Opposite — pH & PaCO₂ move opposite ways
Metabolic Equal — pH & HCO₃⁻ move the same way
disorderpHPaCO₂HCO₃⁻
metabolic acidosis↓normal↓
metabolic alkalosis↑normal↑
respiratory acidosis↓↑normal
respiratory alkalosis↑↓normal
one system fixes the other
lungs — fast "relatively quick"
fix metabolic problems
met acidosis → hyperventilate, blow off CO₂ → pH ↑
met alkalosis → hypoventilate, keep CO₂ → pH ↓
kidneys — slow hours → days
fix respiratory problems — "slow & steady friends, they fix it for good"
resp acidosis → dump H⁺, keep HCO₃⁻ → pH ↑
resp alkalosis → keep H⁺, dump HCO₃⁻ → pH ↓
uncompensated
only the driver moved · other value still normal · pH abnormal
"bicarb is not moving in any direction"
partial
second value is moving the right way · pH still outside 7.35–7.45
"even 7.34 is almost there but not there"
full
pH back in range, both values abnormal · pH leans toward the original disorder
use 7.40 as the midpoint: below = acid side, above = base side
mixed
all three point the same way · two primary problems
COPD + severe diarrhea → "all screaming acid"
a value still inside normal doesn't count as compensation — HCO₃⁻ 23 is normal. "maybe it started at 26, we don't know."
vomiting in DKA is not compensation — it's a side effect. compensation is lungs & kidneys only.
